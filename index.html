Google Sheets basics

You'll use Google Sheets for two things: removing personal data before upload, and checking the AI's numbers.

Open the file

  1. Go to sheets.new
  2. File → Import → Upload, choose the .xlsx
  3. Pick Replace spreadsheet → Import data

Use the tabs at the bottom to switch sheets.

Save it for Claude

  1. File → Download
  2. Microsoft Excel (.xlsx)
  3. Attach that file to Claude

Check a number

  1. Click an empty cell, like M1
  2. Paste the formula from the Check box
  3. Press Enter, compare with Claude

Find anything with Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on Mac).

The six steps of any analysis

Every demo and every hands-on step today is one of these, and each ends with a check.

StepThe question it answersHow you check it
1 · Understand the dataWhat does one row mean, and what can this data answer?Count the rows with =COUNTA
2 · Clean itIs the data clean enough to trust?Recompute one changed row
3 · Answer the questionWhat do the numbers say about the owner's decision?Does it make sense? Then =SUMIF
4 · Chart itCan the owner see the answer in seconds?Bars match your checked totals and start at zero
5 · Read the commentsWhat do customers complain about most?Counts add up; quotes found word for word
6 · ReportWhat should the owner do next?Every number traced to a check

How to ask AI

Why: a vague request like “Analyze this data” lets the AI pick its own question, use bad rows and skip checks.

A good request has four parts. Every prompt on this page is labeled with them.

Role

Who would I hire to do this?

“You are a careful data analyst.”

Task

What exactly do I want back?

One job per request. Start with an action word: describe, find, calculate, chart, sort.

Context

What would a new coworker need to know?

What the data is, what one row means, and the owner's goal.

Constraints

How could this go wrong, and what rule stops it?

Name the mistakes to look for and how numbers are calculated.

Step 6 is different: the AI writes for a person, so the parts become Context, Role, Action, Format and Tone.

Step 2 · Clean the data Demo 2

Why: AI uses bad data without noticing, and it only checks for the problems you name.

First: spot the AI mistake

I asked AI to sum up PhoneHub's 2025 sales. There's one big mistake. What is it, and how would you check it?

MonthSales ($)MonthSales ($)
Jan48,200Jul950,000
Feb46,900Aug53,900
Mar51,300Sep50,700
Apr52,800Oct49,800
May54,100Nov52,400
Jun55,600Dec58,300
What the AI said: “Total 2025 sales were $1,524,000, an average of $127,000 a month. July was a record month at $950,000, about 18 times a typical month. Recommendation: repeat July's summer promotion every year.”
Show the answer

July's $950,000 is an outlier: a value far bigger or smaller than the rest, almost certainly a typing error. The other 11 months are all between $46,900 and $58,300. Because the AI treated July as real, its $127,000 “average” is higher than 11 of the 12 months, and the advice is built on a typo. To catch it, sort the column or chart it, or compare =MAX(B2:B13) with =MEDIAN(B2:B13). Then confirm the real number with whoever owns the data.

Five mistakes to watch for

MistakeWhat it looks likeQuick check
OutlierOne value far bigger or smaller than the rest, used as if it were normalSort the column. Does one value tower over the others?
Missing data filled inA month or row that isn't in the data appears in the answer, with no note that it was estimatedCount the rows. Are they all there?
Duplicate rowsThe same row counted twice, so totals are too highLook for repeated IDs
Totals that don't add upA total that doesn't match the numbers listed above itAdd the parts yourself
Wrong averages or percentages“67% said yes” when only 4 of 12 didCount it: how many out of how many?

Then: find the problems

RoleYou are a careful data analyst. TaskFind problems in the Sales sheet. Don't change anything yet. ContextOne row is one sale, January to June 2026. ConstraintsCheck every row, not a sample. Look for blanks, duplicate Sale_IDs, brand names spelled different ways, dates outside January–June, unusual values, and rows where Net_Sales doesn't equal Price × Quantity × (1 − Discount/100). Show a table: problem, rows, suggested fix.

Ask: what did it miss?

Does every model appear under only one brand? List any model filed under more than one.

Apply the fixes

Apply the fixes. List every row you changed, before and after, and give the new row count.

Check · Recompute one row

Pick one changed row and find its Sale_ID with Ctrl+F. Was the “before” value really there? Then recompute Net_Sales in an empty cell, using that row's number instead of 2:

=G2*H2*(1-I2/100)
Show the problems (6 types)
  • Brand spelled different ways: apple, APPLE, Samsng, “Samsung ” with a space, Moto
  • Pixel 10 filed under Samsung (2 rows). Most people miss this one!
  • One duplicate sale (S-1106)
  • Five blank discounts. We assume 0; write that down, the owner might disagree.
  • Four wrong Net_Sales values
  • One sale dated July 2, outside the period

After cleaning: 126 rows.

Step 3 · Answer the question Demo 3

Why: analysis starts with the business question, not the prompt. If you don't know what you're trying to find out, neither does the AI.

First: turn each question into measures

  1. What will the owner decide?
  2. Which numbers would change that decision?
  3. Which columns hold those numbers?
Jordan asksSo we measureWhat the answer would look like
Which phones should I restock?Which brands and models sell most (units and Net Sales), compared with stock on hand from InventorySelling fast with little stock = restock. Selling slowly with lots of stock = slow down
Are online discounts too deep?In-store vs online: the average discount and the profit margin (the share of each sale that's profit)If online gives bigger discounts and earns less per sale, discounts may be too deep

Then: ask for exactly those measures

RoleYou are an analyst helping the store owner make a decision. TaskShow Net Sales by brand, the top 5 models by units, stock vs sales for each model, and in-store vs online average discount and profit margin. ContextCleaned data. The owner wants to know what to restock and whether online discounts are too deep. ConstraintsUse Net Sales = Price × Quantity × (1 − Discount/100) and profit = Net Sales − Unit cost × Quantity. Match model names between Sales and Inventory and list any that don't match. Tell me how many rows you used. Mark anything that's a guess.
Show the answers
  • Apple is the top brand: $56,293.10 of $116,750.98 total Net Sales.
  • Online discounts average 12.5% vs 3.3% in store, and online profit margin is 12.4% vs 20.2%.
  • Restock iPhone 17 (3 left, 29 sold). Slow down Galaxy Z Flip7 (34 left, 4 sold).
  • Name mismatch: Inventory says “Galaxy Z Flip 7”, Sales says “Galaxy Z Flip7”.

Does the answer make sense?

A formula proves the AI added correctly, not that the answer is reasonable. Spend 30 seconds on these first.

AskPhoneHub example
Do the parts add up to the total?Net Sales by brand adds to $116,750.98
Were any rows lost?Rows used = 126, the count after cleaning
Is every number possible?Margins and discounts fall between 0% and 100%
Do related numbers point the same way?Online: deeper discounts and a thinner margin. That fits.
Does one item take an odd share?Apple at 48% is plausible for a phone store. At 90%, look for an outlier.

Check · The numbers in Google Sheets

Open phonehub_clean.xlsx in Google Sheets. Check the numbers the decision rests on, and write each checked number down: step 6 uses only these.

To checkFormulaShould match
How many rows=COUNTA(A:A)-1126

Using AI responsibly

Why: AI makes analysis fast, but you're responsible for what goes into the tool and for every number that comes out.

Privacy

Remove names, emails, phone numbers and ID numbers before you upload, including ones hidden in free text. At work, use only AI tools your organization approves.

Accuracy

You've seen how confidently AI can be wrong, and how to catch it. Check every number before you share it.

Bias

A survey of online shoppers describes them, not all customers. Use AI for patterns in groups, never to judge one named person.

You own it

If your name is on the report, you're responsible for its numbers, not the AI.

What can I share with an AI tool?

Green: fine to shareAmber: approved tool only, personal details removedRed: never share
Column names and data types · made-up data like today's · public information · totals with no individuals · your own prompts and formulasInternal sales by product or store · customer feedback with names removed · supplier costs · unreleased plansNames with phones, emails or addresses · ID, bank or card numbers · salaries or reviews of named staff · health details · passwords · customer lists · anything under NDA

Before you use AI on real work data, tick all four

  • This tool is approved by my organization for this kind of data.
  • I removed names, contact details, IDs and anything confidential, including in free text.
  • I checked the key numbers, and marked guesses as guesses.
  • My report says AI assisted, and names who checked it.
