Last session: role, task, context, tone, constraints. For data, swap tone for something data needs more: proof. Every data prompt has four parts, and they spell the subject.

Using AI responsibly

Why: AI makes analysis fast, but you are still responsible for what goes into the tool and for every number that comes out.

Five habits. You'll practice every one of them today.

1 · Protect

Remove before you upload

Names, phones, emails, IDs, and anything hiding in free text. A screenshot counts as an upload.

2 · Approve

Right tool for the data

Real work data only goes into a tool your organization has approved. Today's data is fake, so any tool is fine.

3 · Verify

Check before you share

Use CHECK on every number you'll pass on. No check, no send.

4 · Label

Fact or guess?

AI can calculate. It can't know why people did something.

5 · Disclose

Say AI helped

Your report names AI as a helper and you as the person who verified it.

What can I share with an AI tool?

Green: fine to shareAmber: approved tool only, personal details removedRed: never share
Column names and data types · synthetic data like today's · public information · totals with no individuals · your own prompts and formulasInternal sales by product or store · customer feedback with names removed · supplier costs · unreleased plansNames with phones, emails or addresses · ID, bank or card numbers · salaries or reviews of named staff · health details · passwords · customer lists · anything under NDA

Need the data but not the person? Five ways to make it safer

  1. Remove columns you don't need (today: Customer_Name, Phone, Email).
  2. Mask identifiers with codes (Jessica Miller → CUST-0042) and keep the key offline.
  3. Generalize: exact age → age band; street address → city.
  4. Aggregate: daily totals instead of individual orders.
  5. Rehearse on fake data, then run the method on real data only in an approved tool, and verify again.

Label every statement before you share it

LabelMeansExample
CalculationA number the tool computed. Check it.Apple Net Sales = $56,293.10
FindingA pattern in the numbersOnline sells at deeper discounts
HypothesisA possible reason. AI cannot know it.Online buyers may be more price-driven
RecommendationAn action for a person to decide and testCap online discounts at 8% for two weeks

Stop and verify when…

  • a result is surprisingly good or bad
  • percentages don't add to 100, or counts don't add to the total
  • the AI explains “why” but no column records why
  • a quote sounds too perfect (Ctrl+F it)
  • two tools disagree
  • you're about to send it to your boss

Fairness: use AI to find patterns in groups, such as stores, products and categories. Never use it to judge a named person, like a customer or a staff member.

Part 1 · PhoneHub, guided

Spot the Bad Number 5 minOn your own · no tools

Why: AI numbers can be confidently wrong. Learn to spot a bad one before you touch any tool.

Three AI answers about PhoneHub. Which would you trust?

Answer A

“Total Net Sales for Jan–Jun 2026 was $118,973.68 across 128 sales.”

Answer B

“Online discounts averaged 12.5%, because online shoppers are more price-sensitive.”

Answer C

“Using the 126 cleaned rows, Apple Net Sales = $56,293.10 from 71 units.”

  1. Read each answer.
  2. Decide: trust it, doubt it, or check it? Write a word next to each.
  3. Which would hurt you most if you sent it to your boss? Be ready to say why.
Show the answers
  • A: wrong scope. It counted every raw row, including a duplicate, a July sale and four wrong values. The right total is $116,750.98 from 126 sales.
  • B: the number is right, the “because” is invented. No column records why people buy. That's a guess dressed up as a fact.
  • C: trust it after a check. It says which rows it used, so you can verify it.

Meet CHECK, your verification habit

Confirm the scope · Hand-check one · Expose the method · Cross-check · Keep a log

Step 1 · Describe 5 min

Why: if you and the AI disagree on what one row means, every number after it is wrong.
I'm an analyst for an independent phone retailer. I've attached our file with three sheets. For each sheet: list every column with 3 sample values, tell me in plain language what one row means, and give the date range and row count.

Verify · Confirm scope

In Google Sheets, on each tab, paste this into an empty cell. It counts filled rows minus the header. Does it match Claude?

=COUNTA(A:A)-1
Show the answer

One Sales row = one sale of one model (quantity 1–3) at one store on one date. Sales has 128 rows, Inventory 13, Reviews 40. Inventory shows stock on one date, Jun 30, 2026.

Stretch: ask Which column would you not trust without checking, and why?

Step 2 · Check and clean 7 min

Why: messy data produces confident wrong answers, and AI only finds the problems you ask it to look for.

First, find the problems

Run a data quality check on the Sales sheet. Check every row, not a sample. Look for: missing values, duplicate Sale_IDs, inconsistent brand spellings, dates outside Jan–Jun 2026, and rows where Net_Sales does not equal Price_USD × Quantity × (1 − Discount_Pct/100). Show a table: problem | rows | suggested fix. Don't change anything yet.

Then ask: what did it miss?

Does every model appear under only one brand? List any model filed under more than one.

Now apply the fixes

Apply these fixes: standardize brand names, put each model under its correct brand, remove the duplicate sale, set blank discounts to 0, exclude sales outside Jan–Jun 2026, and recalculate Net_Sales for every row. List every row you changed with before and after values, then give the new row count.

Verify · Hand-check one

Pick one changed row. In Google Sheets press Ctrl+F and search its Sale_ID. Was the “before” value really there? Then recompute Net_Sales in an empty cell, using that row's number instead of 2:

=G2*H2*(1-I2/100)
Show the problems (there are 6 types)
  • Brand spelled different ways: apple, APPLE, Samsng, “Samsung ” with a space, Moto
  • Pixel 10 filed under Samsung (2 rows). Most people miss this one!
  • One duplicate sale (S-1106)
  • Five blank discounts. We assume 0; write that down, the owner might disagree.
  • Four wrong Net_Sales values
  • One sale dated July 2, outside the period

After cleaning: 126 rows.

Step 3 · Ask better questions 5 min

Why: AI widens your thinking with questions you didn't consider, and shows what the data can't tell you.
Suggest 10 questions the owner could answer with this data, and say in one line why each matters.

Pick the three that interest you most. Fill in their numbers:

For questions [#, #, #]: which columns do you need, and is the data enough to answer each? Then: what would the owner want to know that this data cannot answer?
What to notice

No column records why customers bought, foot traffic, staff costs or returns. Anything about “why” is a Hypothesis, not a Finding.

Step 4 · Check the join 3 minWatch

Why: combining two sheets silently drops rows when names don't match exactly.
How do the Sales and Inventory sheets connect? Which column would you use to join them, and list any values that don't match between the two sheets.
Show the answer

Sales says Galaxy Z Flip7; Inventory says Galaxy Z Flip 7. One space, and that model vanishes from the restock analysis. It's the model with the most unsold stock. A join is a hypothesis: always ask which values don't match.

Step 5 · Analyze with DATA 7 min

Why: a clear prompt turns a generic summary into an answer you can act on and check.

Your instructor runs the bad and good prompts. Guess what's missing from each before you see the result.

Bad

Analyze this data.

The AI picks its own questions and probably uses all 128 raw rows. You can't check anything.

Good

Which brand sold the most, and which channel gives bigger discounts? Show the answers in tables.

Answers your question, but the AI decides what “sold the most” means (units or dollars?) and which rows count.

Best: you run this one. It has all four DATA parts.

[D] I'm an analyst for PhoneHub Mobile, a phone retailer with two stores and an online shop. In the Sales sheet one row = one sale, Jan–Jun 2026. Use the cleaned data and match the Galaxy Z Flip7 names between sheets. [A] My goal: help the owner decide which models to restock and whether online discounts are too deep. [T] Answer with small tables: 1. Net Sales by brand 2. Top 5 models by units sold 3. In-store vs Online: units, Net Sales, average discount %, gross margin % 4. Models needing attention: high stock + low sales, or low stock + high sales Net Sales = Price_USD × Quantity × (1 − Discount_Pct/100). Gross Profit = Net Sales − Unit_Cost_USD × Quantity. [A] Format money as US dollars. Tell me how many rows you used and the formula behind each number, and label each statement Calculation, Finding or Hypothesis.

Verify · Cross-check

Open phonehub_clean.xlsx in Google Sheets. Paste each formula into an empty cell. Both must match Claude.

Apple Net Sales

=SUMIF(E:E,"Apple",K:K)

Units sold online

=SUMIF(D:D,"Online",H:H)

Don't match? Ask Claude: Show the exact filters and rows you used.

Show the answers
  • Apple Net Sales: $56,293.10 (top brand)
  • Units sold online: 42
  • Online discounts average 12.5% vs 3.3% in store, and online margin is 12.4% vs 20.2%.
  • Restock: iPhone 17 (3 left, 29 sold). Too much stock: Galaxy Z Flip7 (34 left, 4 sold).

Stretch: Which brand's share differs most between Atlanta and Denver?
