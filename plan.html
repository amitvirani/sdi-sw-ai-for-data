AI for Data: 90-Minute Session Plan

The six steps of any analysis: you demonstrate them on PhoneHub, then everyone does them on Corner Café.

Session planTrainer guideParticipant page

At a glance

90 minutes, 25 slides, built on the six steps of any analysis: understand the data, clean it, answer the question, chart it, read the comments, report. You demonstrate each step on PhoneHub, a phone store, while learners watch. Then learners spend 47 minutes (52%) doing the same six steps on their own with Corner Café, a coffee shop. PhoneHub is only for the demos; Corner Café is only for the hands-on.

AudienceMixed: some confident, some know basic AI tools, some digital literate but slow. Everyone works on their own; there is no pair or group work.
ToolsClaude (one chat per dataset) · Google Sheets (privacy edits and checks)
ColorsPages and slides use the same strip colors: navy = Learn (explanations and demos), teal = Hands-on. Amber marks a no-tool activity and appears only in the slide legend.

Learning objectives Slide 3

By the end of the session, participants can:

  1. Remove personal details from a file before they upload it, including details hidden in free text.
  2. Turn a business question into things they can measure.
  3. Use AI to understand, clean and analyze a spreadsheet, and chart the answer.
  4. Spot AI mistakes: judge whether a result makes sense, and check its numbers in Google Sheets.
  5. Sort customer comments into themes.
  6. Write a short report for a manager using only checked numbers.

How to ask AI Slide 5

One slide, inside Demo 1. After it, every demo just labels its prompt's parts; there's no lesson on the letters.

PartAsk yourselfIn Demo 1

CRAFT (Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone) appears only inside Demo 6, because the report is the one step where AI writes for a person. If your deck uses different words for any letter, keep yours.

Materials and prep

The day before

  • Run every PhoneHub prompt in one Claude chat; compare with the trainer guide's answer key; screenshot each output as a fallback
  • Run the Corner Café hands-on as a participant would, including the Google Sheets checks; time it
  • Publish the participant page with its files/ folder; test every download link on a laptop
  • Check the venue wifi with the whole room on AI tools at once
  • Plan seating: put slower participants on aisle seats near the front so you can reach them quickly

Send participants before class

  • Bring a laptop, not a phone: you'll have Google Sheets and Claude side by side.
  • Sign in to Claude and to a Google account (for Google Sheets) on that laptop before class.
  • Claude Pro is ideal. On the free plan, long chats can hit usage limits; the clean backup files let anyone restart in a fresh chat.

Mixed-ability support

Priority check-ins

In the hands-on, visit slower participants first at H1 (file setup) and H3 (measures and checks), where most people get stuck.

Fallbacks

Stuck more than 2 minutes after H2? Clean file, new chat, rejoin at H3. Screenshots cover a failed demo.

Fast finishers

Compare Austin and Nashville, or build a simple dashboard and check one number on it against Google Sheets.

If you're running behind, cut in this order

  1. Demo 4: show your screenshot of the charts instead of generating them
  2. Demo 5: label two reviews with the room instead of five
  3. Slide 8: point to the table on the participant page and move on
  4. Never cut slide 7 (spot the mistake), slides 11–12 (sense and Sheets checks) or H6

Take-home Slide 25

About 45 minutes. Participants finish the Corner Café analysis they started, so nobody faces a new file alone.

  • Finish any H1–H6 steps
  • Two charts
  • Austin vs Nashville: one finding with a number
  • A checks log with 4+ checks: two numbers recomputed in Google Sheets, one quote check, one totals check
  • One-page summary for Maya, with the AI-assistance line and their name
CriterionPointsFull marks look like
Responsible AI and checking30Personal data removed; 4+ logged checks; guesses marked; AI use disclosed
Accuracy20Numbers match the key within $1, using the stated cleaning rules
Data quality15All planted problems found, including the price outlier and the name mismatch
Insights and recommendations20Specific, numeric, combine sales and reviews, each with a 2-week test
Charts15Right chart type, titled with the finding, axes in $ or %, bars from zero

A wrong number your own check caught and fixed scores higher than a right number with no check.
