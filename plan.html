AI for Data: 90-Minute Session Plan

Lead PhoneHub on screen, then everyone repeats the recipe on their own with Copper Kettle Coffee.

Session planTrainer guideParticipant page

At a glance

90 minutes, three moves. You analyze PhoneHub Mobile on screen while everyone follows along. Then each person repeats the same recipe on their own, on a new business, Copper Kettle Coffee, with less help. Same prompts, same checks, different data, so the hands-on proves they learned the method, not the answers.

AudienceMixed: some confident, some know basic AI tools, some digital literate but slow. Every step has a core path everyone finishes and a stretch for fast finishers.

Learning objectives

By the end of the session, participants can:

  1. Remove personal data from a file before it goes into an AI tool, including details hidden in free text.
  2. Describe a dataset with AI and confirm what one row means.
  3. Find and fix data-quality problems, and list what changed.
  4. Ask AI for better questions, and for what the data can't answer.
  5. Write data prompts with the DATA framework: Describe, Aim, Task + Terms, Answer + Audit.
  6. Turn free-text reviews into categories, sentiment and themes, and measure agreement.
  7. Verify AI results with CHECK in Google Sheets, and write a summary that separates facts from guesses.

Five key moments

What participants should see for themselves at each stage, and where it happens.

1 · One prompt finds the mess

Opening demo and Step 2: Claude spots problems in seconds, then misses one you didn't ask about.

2 · Better questions

Step 3: AI suggests angles you didn't think of, and admits what the data can't answer.

3 · The join is a hypothesis

Step 4: one space in a model name quietly drops data.

4 · It reads 40 reviews

Step 6: sarcasm and Spanish included, and you measure how often it agrees with you.

5 · Your own finding

Dashboard demo, then the hands-on, where each person finds something themselves.

The DATA prompt framework

Builds on last session's role, task, context, tone and constraints, with tone swapped for what data needs more: proof. The letters spell the subject of the class.

LetterStands forWhat you tell the AIExample
DDescribeWho you are; which sheet; what one row means; which period; cleaned or raw“I'm an analyst for a phone retailer. One Sales row = one sale, Jan–Jun 2026.”
AAimThe decision the answer will feed“My goal: decide what to restock and whether online discounts are too deep.”
TTask + TermsThe exact questions, and how each number is defined“Net Sales by brand. Net Sales = Price × Quantity × (1 − Discount/100).”
AAnswer + AuditThe format, and the proof: rows used, formulas, fact vs guess“Small tables. Say how many rows you used. Label Calculation, Finding or Hypothesis.”

Describe the data · Aim at a decision · define the Task · Audit the answer

Bad → good → best, run live in Step 5

Bad

Analyze this data.

Generic tour; AI picks its own questions and probably sums all 128 raw rows.

Good

Which brand sold the most, and which channel gives bigger discounts? Show the answers in tables.

Answers the question, but AI decides what “most” means and which rows count, silently.

Best: the full four-part prompt on the participant page, Step 5. In the hands-on, each person rebuilds “Tell me about our coffee sales.” with DATA themselves.

Verification habit: CHECK

Confirm scope · Hand-check one · Expose the method · Cross-check · Keep a log

Using AI responsibly

Responsible use isn't a separate lecture. It's taught as five habits, each practiced at a specific point in the session and assessed in the take-home.

Protect

Remove personal data before upload, including details hidden in free text.

Approve

Real work data goes only into tools the organization has approved.

Verify

CHECK every number before it's shared.

Label

Separate Calculation, Finding, Hypothesis and Recommendation.

Disclose

Reports say AI assisted and name the human verifier.

Where each habit is practiced

HabitPracticed inWhat participants do
ProtectSetup (9–15), H1Delete three personal columns in Google Sheets; find a staff name and an email (PhoneHub), a staff name and a phone number (Copper Kettle) hidden in review text
ApproveThree rules (9–15), green/amber/red quick check, exit ticketSort seven examples into green, amber, red; commit to synthetic or approved data for next week's try

LabelStep 3, Step 5, H4, H6See that “why” questions can't be answered from the data; label each statement in the DATA prompt's Audit line
DiscloseH6 summary, take-homeEnd the owner summary with an AI-assistance line and their name as verifier

Also covered

  • Fairness: analyze groups (stores, products, categories), never judge named individuals with AI.
  • Made-up content: the review quote check shows AI can invent a quote that sounds real.
  • Human accountability: the person who signs the summary owns its numbers, not the tool.

Assessment: “Responsible AI and verification” is the largest take-home criterion, at 30 of 100 points.

Materials and prep

The day before

  • Run every PhoneHub prompt in one Claude chat; compare with the trainer guide's answer key; screenshot each output as a fallback
  • Run the Copper Kettle core path as a participant would, including the Google Sheets steps; time it
  • Build the PhoneHub dashboard once and save the HTML as the backup dashboard
  • Publish the participant page with its files/ folder; test every download link on a phone and a laptop
  • Print the Spot the Bad Number cards (one per person), or just use the participant page
  • Check the venue wifi with the whole room on AI tools at once
  • Plan seating: put slower participants on aisle seats near the front so you can reach them quickly

Send participants before class

  • Bring a laptop, not a phone: you'll have Google Sheets and Claude side by side.
  • Sign in to Claude and to a Google account (for Google Sheets) on that laptop before class.
  • Claude Pro is ideal. On the free plan, long chats can hit usage limits; the clean backup files let anyone restart in a fresh chat.

Mixed-ability support

Priority check-ins

Everyone works on their own. In the hands-on, visit slower participants first, at H1 (file setup) and H3 (cleaning), where most people get stuck.

Fallbacks

Lost after Step 2 or H3? Clean file, new chat, rejoin at the next step. Backup dashboard covers a failed demo.

Fast finishers

Every step has a Stretch line; in Part C they take the stretch list, then try the dashboard or the recompute check.

If you're running behind, cut in this order

  1. Step 4 (join): one sentence on screen, keep it as a hands-on stretch
  2. Step 3: run only “what can't this data answer?”
  3. Dashboard: open the backup instead of generating
  4. Never cut Step 2's “what did it miss?” moment or the H6 summary

Take-home

About 45 minutes. Participants finish the Copper Kettle analysis they started, so nobody faces a new file alone.

  • Finish any H1–H6 steps
  • Run the join check and explain the mismatch and fix
  • Austin vs Nashville: one finding with a number
  • Two charts
  • Verification log with 4+ checks: two numbers recomputed (one in Google Sheets), one quote check, one totals check
  • One-page summary for Maya, with AI-use disclosure and verifier name
