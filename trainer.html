Part A · Basics 0–5 min

Slides 1–4: the promise, the six steps, the goals, and where AI slips.

Title, outline, goals 0–4Slides 1–3

Say“Today you'll use AI to answer real business questions from a spreadsheet, then check every number before you share it. Every analysis follows six steps: understand the data, clean it, answer the question, chart it, read the comments, report. You'll watch me do all six on a phone store, then do them yourself on a coffee shop.”
DoPoint out the color strip: navy = watch, teal = your own laptop. All data is made up, so it's safe to upload anywhere.

What AI does well, and where it slips 4–5Slide 4

Say“It reads a whole spreadsheet in seconds. It also trusts bad data, fills gaps quietly, and sounds just as sure when it's wrong. You'll see these slips in the demos.”

Part B · Watch on PhoneHub 5–36 min

Slides 5–15. You run each step; learners watch. Each step ends with a check.

Step 1 · How to ask AI 5–7Slide 5

Why: a vague request like “Analyze this data” lets the AI pick its own question, use bad rows and skip checks.
Say“A good request has four parts. Who would I hire? What exactly do I want back? What would a new coworker need to know? How could this go wrong, and what rule stops it? Every demo labels its prompt with these, so you won't need to memorize the letters.”
NoteThis is the only slide on RTCC. Don't teach the letters again later; just point at the labels.

Demo 1 · Understand the data 7–10Slide 6

Why: if you think one row is one customer but it's really one sale, every number after that is wrong.
DoProtect first, live: open phonehub_sales.xlsx in Google Sheets (File → Import → Upload → Replace spreadsheet). In Reviews, delete Customer_Name, Phone and Email; replace the staff name “Marcus” in R-01 and the email in R-22. Download as .xlsx, attach to a new Claude chat, paste the Demo 1 prompt.
Say“The columns were the easy part. Personal details hide in free text.”
ExpectOne Sales row = one sale of one model, at one store, on one date. 5 questions, each naming its columns. Can't tell us why customers bought.
Check=COUNTA(A:A)-1 on each tab: 128, 13, 40.
Watch forClaude reading only one sheet → Please read all three sheets.

Demo 2 · Spot the AI mistake 10–14Slide 7

Why: before cleaning anything, learners see for themselves that AI can be confidently wrong.
DoGive the room about 60 seconds: “There's one big mistake. What is it, and how would you check it?” Take two or three answers, then click to reveal the answer panel.
ExpectJuly's $950,000 is an outlier, almost certainly a typing error (an extra digit). The other 11 months are $46,900–$58,300 and average $52,182. The AI's other numbers are correct (the total really is $1,524,000), so the outlier is the only problem; its $127,000 average is above 11 of 12 months and the advice is built on a typo.
Say“Sort the column or chart it, and one value towers over the rest. In Google Sheets, compare =MAX with =MEDIAN. Then confirm the real number with whoever owns the data.”

Demo 2 · Five mistakes to watch for 14–16Slide 8

Say“You've just caught an outlier. Here are all five, and the quick check for each. These go straight into the cleaning request.”
ExpectOutlier · missing data filled in · duplicate rows · totals that don't add up · wrong averages or percentages.

Demo 2 · Clean the data 16–19Slide 9

Why: AI only checks for the problems you name.
DoRun the find-problems prompt. Before the follow-up, stop the room.
Say“It found a lot. What did it miss?” Then run Does every model appear under only one brand? “Two Pixel 10 sales were filed under Samsung. It didn't find them because we didn't ask.”
ThenRun the fix prompt. “Setting blank discounts to 0 is our assumption, not a fact. Write it down.” Define Net Sales: the money received after the discount.
Expect126 rows after cleaning. Full change list in the PhoneHub key.
CheckCtrl+F one changed Sale_ID, then =G2*H2*(1-I2/100) with that row's number.
Watch for“No issues found” → Check every row, not a sample.

Demo 3 · Answer the question 19–24Slide 10

Why: analysis starts with the business question, not the prompt.
DoLive, before revealing the measures table, ask the room: “What will Jordan decide? Which numbers would change that decision? Which columns hold them?” Then reveal the table and run the prompt.
ExpectApple top brand $56,293.10. Online discounts 12.5% vs 3.3% in store; online margin 12.4% vs 20.2%. Restock iPhone 17 (3 left, 29 sold); slow down Galaxy Z Flip7 (34 left, 4 sold). Name mismatch: “Galaxy Z Flip 7” in Inventory.
Say“Before Jordan sees any of this, we check it two ways: does it make sense, and do the numbers match Google Sheets?”

Does the answer make sense? 24–26Slide 11

DoRun the five questions out loud on Demo 3's answer. Parts add to $116,750.98; 126 rows used; every % between 0 and 100; deeper discount with thinner margin points the same way; Apple at 48% is plausible.
Ask the room“Which question would have caught July's $950,000 on slide 7?” (Does one item take an odd share?)
Say“If something fails, ask the AI to show its working for that number before you change anything.”

Check the numbers in Google Sheets 26–28Slide 12

DoOn phonehub_clean.xlsx, run two or three in empty cells: =COUNTA(A:A)-1 → 126 · =SUMIF(E:E,"Apple",K:K) → 56293.1 · =SUMIF(D:D,"Online",H:H) → 42 · =AVERAGEIF(D:D,"Online",I:I) → 12.53.
Say“Check the numbers the decision rests on, the ones going in the report, and write each one down. Demo 6 uses only these.”
Watch forAn average that differs: ask the AI whether it averaged per row or per unit sold. Both can be right; the report must say which.

Demo 4 · Chart it 28–31Slide 13

Why: a chart shows the pattern at a glance, and a bad number jumps out.
DoPick the chart first (bar high to low for comparing items; line for over time; side-by-side bars for two groups), then run the prompt.
Say“A title that states the finding, like ‘Apple brings in nearly half of sales’, tells the reader what to look at. Bars that start at zero stop small differences from looking huge.”
ExpectApple's bar $56,293.10, about 48% of $116,750.98. Online discount bar (12.5%) almost four times in-store (3.3%).
If it stallsShow your screenshot, or select a small table of totals in Google Sheets and use Insert → Chart.

Demo 5 · Read the comments 31–34Slide 14

Why: the numbers show what happened; reviews hint at why.
DoLabel R-01 to R-05 with the room first (R-02 sarcastic, R-03 Spanish, R-05 mixed), then run the prompt.
ExpectCounts total 40. Top complaints: online shipping, price and trade-in, activation and setup.
Watch forCounts not totaling 40 → Your counts total __, not 40. Show any reviews you skipped. A quote that doesn't Ctrl+F word for word is made up: a great teaching moment.

Demo 6 · Report 34–36Slide 15

Why: the owner wants what the numbers mean and what to do.
Say“This is the one step where AI writes for a person, so the parts change: Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone. The report can only be as good as the checked numbers you put in its Context.”
DoRun the prompt in the same PhoneHub chat. Delete any number you didn't check; add “Prepared with AI assistance; numbers checked by [your name].”
CheckPoint at each number. Can you show where you checked it?

Wrap-up 83–90Slides 23–25

ResponsiblePrivacy (today's data is made up; real work data only in approved tools, after removing personal details, including in comment text) · Accuracy (point back to the outlier, the duplicate and the name mismatch) · Bias (a survey of online shoppers describes them, not all customers) · You own it (their name is on the summary).
Share-out2–3 volunteers each say one finding about Corner Café and how they checked it. Fish for the price outlier (CC-2073), the Colombia Huila name mismatch, or a review where stars and words disagree (C-18, C-21).

Answer keys

Computed in code from the files. Cleaning rules: standardize names; move misfiled items; remove the exact duplicate; blank discounts = 0; exclude dates outside Jan–Jun 2026; fix the price outlier; recalculate Net_Sales = Price_USD × Quantity × (1 − Discount_Pct/100). Average discount = simple average per sale. Profit margin = (Net Sales − Unit_Cost_USD × Quantity) ÷ Net Sales.

H6 · Report

A strong summary uses only checked numbers. For example: online discounts are five times deeper than in store (15.0% vs 3.0%), with a lower margin (49.7% vs 56.6%); the top seller, Colombia Huila, is nearly out of stock (5 left, 37 sold); and most complaints are about subscriptions and online orders. Each recommendation comes with a 2-week test, and the summary ends with the AI-assistance line and the learner's name.
