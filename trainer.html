Teaching responsible AI use

Why: participants will take these habits to work with real data, where a leaked customer list or an unchecked number has real consequences.

Don't lecture it all at the start. Introduce the five habits in Part A, then name the habit each time it comes up. Participants have the full reference in the “Using AI responsibly” section of their page.

Five habits, in one breath (Three rules segment, 9–15)

Say“Five habits today. Protect: remove personal data before upload. Approve: real work data only in an approved tool. Verify: check every number. Label: fact or guess. Disclose: say AI helped and who checked it. You'll practice all five before you leave.”
DoIf time allows, run the green/amber/red quick check on their page out loud (2 min); otherwise assign it for the take-home. Answers: 1 Green, 2 Amber, 3 Green, 4 Red, 5 Amber, 6 Red, 7 Green.

Questions people usually ask

“Can I use this at work?”Only with tools and data your organization has approved. Ask IT or your manager what's allowed before you upload anything real. Don't guess.
“Does the AI keep my data?”It depends on the tool, the plan and its settings, and it can change. Point people to their organization's policy and the tool's own data settings rather than promising either way. When unsure, share less: describe the data instead of uploading it.
“Isn't removing names enough?”Not always. A rare job title plus a small store can identify someone. Generalize or aggregate when you only need patterns.
“If the AI is usually right, why check?”Point back to Spot the Bad Number: Answer A looked right and was off by $2,222.70. One unchecked number can undo a whole report.
“Can I use it to review my team?”Don't use AI to judge named individuals. Use it for patterns across groups, and keep people decisions with people.

Step in if you see

  • Someone uploading a real work file “just to try it” → stop them kindly, and point to the five-point workplace checklist on their page.
  • A summary with a “because” and no label → ask “Calculation, Finding or Hypothesis?”
  • Someone copying Claude's summary without checking → “Show me where you verified that number.”

Part A · Warm-up 0–15 min

A quick demo, a no-tool puzzle, then setup step by step.

Opening demo: one prompt finds the problems 0–4You only

Why: show what one prompt can do in 30 seconds, so everyone wants to learn how.
DoAttach phonehub_sales.xlsx to Claude and run:
Run a quick data quality check on the Sales sheet. What are the 3 biggest problems you can see?
Say“That took 20 seconds. By the end of class you'll do this yourself, and you'll know when not to trust it.”
ExpectSome of: brand spellings (apple, Samsng, Moto), duplicate S-1106, blank discounts, July sale. If nothing clear comes back, show your screenshot. Don't explain yet.

Spot the Bad Number 4–9On their own

Why: AI numbers can be confidently wrong, so learn to spot a bad one before touching any tool.
DoEveryone reads the cards on their own (also on the participant page) for two minutes, then take answers from the room.
Say“Which one would hurt you most if you sent it to your boss?”
ExpectA wrong scope: raw total $118,973.68 from 128 rows vs correct $116,750.98 from 126. B number right, “because” invented. C trust after a check. Then introduce CHECK in one breath: Confirm scope, Hand-check one, Expose the method, Cross-check, Keep a log.

Five habits + setup 9–15Everyone

Why: personal data must be removed before it reaches an AI tool, and this is the habit they take to work.
SayIntroduce the five habits using the script in Teaching responsible AI use (1 min), then go straight into setup.
DoClick through setup slowly on the projector, pausing after each step until most hands are up:
  1. Download from the participant page → sheets.new → File → Import → Upload → Replace spreadsheet
  2. Reviews tab: right-click column letters for Customer_Name, Phone, Email → Delete column
  3. Fix R-01 (staff name “Marcus”) and R-22 (an email) → [staff], [email]
  4. File → Download → Microsoft Excel (.xlsx)
  5. New Claude chat → attach → wait for the file chip
Watch forPeople attaching the original download instead of their Sheets export. Quick check in Claude: Does the Reviews sheet have Name, Phone or Email columns? If yes, wrong file. Import dialog defaulting to “Insert new sheet(s)”. Mac users needing Cmd instead of Ctrl.

Part B · Guided practice on PhoneHub 15–55 min

Every step: you show it, they run it, everyone verifies one thing before moving on.

Step 1 · Describe 15–20

Why: if you and the AI disagree on what one row means, every number after it is wrong.
Say“This is the D in DATA: Describe. Before analysis, make sure you and the AI agree on what one row is.”
ExpectOne Sales row = one sale of one model (qty 1–3) at one store on one date. Sales 128, Inventory 13, Reviews 40. Inventory = stock on Jun 30, 2026, not a running total.
Verify=COUNTA(A:A)-1 on each tab in Google Sheets.
Watch forClaude reading only one sheet → Please read all three sheets.

Step 2 · Check and clean 20–27

Why: messy data produces confident wrong answers, and AI only finds the problems you ask it to look for.
DoRun the check prompt. Before the follow-up, stop the room.
Say“It found a lot. What did it miss?” Let them guess, then run Does every model appear under only one brand? “Two Pixel 10 sales were filed under Samsung. It didn't find them because we didn't ask.”
ThenRun the fix prompt. “Setting blank discounts to 0 is our assumption. Write it down; the owner might disagree.”
Expect126 rows after cleaning. Full change list in the PhoneHub key.
VerifyHand-check one changed row: Ctrl+F its Sale_ID, then =G2*H2*(1-I2/100) with that row number.
Watch for“No issues found” → Check every row, not a sample. Anyone lost: clean file, new chat, rejoin at Step 3.

Step 3 · Ask better questions 27–32

Why: AI widens your thinking with questions you didn't consider, and shows what the data can't tell you.
DoRun the 10-questions prompt; everyone picks the 3 that interest them and runs the follow-up with their own numbers. On the projector, use 3 you choose.
Say“Anything about why customers did something is a Hypothesis. No column records why.”
ExpectCan't answer: reasons for buying, foot traffic, staff costs, returns, customer demographics.

Step 4 · Check the join 32–35Demo only

Why: combining two sheets silently drops rows when names don't match exactly.
ExpectJoin on Model. Mismatch: Inventory Galaxy Z Flip 7 vs Sales Galaxy Z Flip7. That's the model with the most unsold stock (34 on hand, 4 sold), so a naive join hides exactly the problem the owner needs to see.
Say“A join is a hypothesis. Always ask which values don't match.”
If behindCut this to one sentence; it's a stretch in the hands-on.

Step 5 · DATA: bad → good → best 35–42

Why: a clear prompt turns a generic summary into an answer you can act on and check.
DoOn the projector, run Analyze this data. (point out: it picked its own questions, and which rows did it use?). Then the Good prompt (point out: “sold the most” in units or dollars?). Then the room runs Best from the participant page.
Say“Same data, three answers. The only thing that changed is what we gave the AI. Which one could you defend to your boss? D-A-T-A: Describe the data, Aim at a decision, define the Task, Audit the answer. A vague ask gets a vague answer, like briefing an intern.”
ExpectApple top brand $56,293.10; Online 42 units, 12.5% average discount, 12.4% margin vs 20.2% in store; restock iPhone 17 and Pixel 10a; too much stock in Galaxy Z Flip7, Razr 2026, OnePlus 15.
VerifyEveryone opens phonehub_clean.xlsx in Sheets: =SUMIF(E:E,"Apple",K:K) → 56293.1; =SUMIF(D:D,"Online",H:H) → 42.
Watch forTotals off by $799 → the July sale wasn't excluded. Off by one sale → duplicate kept. Ask: Did you remove the duplicate and the July sale?

Step 6 · Read the reviews 42–50

Why: AI reads hundreds of comments in seconds, but you measure how far to trust it.
DoTwo minutes of silent hand-labeling on the participant page (R-01 to R-05), then run the prompt.
Say“Where you and Claude disagree, who's right? There isn't always one answer; the point is to know how far to trust it.”
ExpectR-02 sarcastic, R-03 Spanish, R-05 mixed. Claude usually gets all three. Counts total 40. Top complaints: Online shipping, Price & trade-in, Activation & setup.
Watch forCounts not totaling 40 → Your counts total __, not 40. Show any reviews you skipped. Quotes that don't Ctrl+F word for word → made-up quote, a great teaching moment.

Dashboard + pushback 50–55You

Why: turn verified numbers into something the owner can explore, and prepare for their questions.
DoRun the dashboard prompt. While it generates (1–2 min): “Same data, same formulas. A dashboard can still be wrong.” Set Brand = Apple; the room calls out whether the Net Sales card says $56,293.10. Then run the pushback prompt.
If it stallsOpen the backup dashboard. Don't wait more than 90 seconds.

Part C · Hands-on on Copper Kettle 55–85 min

Everyone works on their own. You circulate.

Release the room 55

Why: same recipe, new data. Proof they learned the method, not the answers.
Say“Same steps, new business, on your own. Run each prompt in Claude, then do its check in Google Sheets. At H4 you'll rebuild a bad prompt with DATA yourself. Stuck for more than two minutes? Raise your hand, or use the clean file and move on.”
DoPut the H1–H6 list on the projector with times. Set a visible 30-minute timer.

Wrap-up 85–90

Why: saying a finding aloud locks it in, and the exit ticket turns it into next week's action.
Ask“What's one finding you didn't expect, and how did you check it?” Take 2–3 volunteers.
Fish forThe price typo, the Colombia name mismatch, a review where stars and words disagree (C-18, C-21).
Close“Rehearse on fake data. Run on real data only in an approved tool. And verify again.” Exit ticket on the page; mention the take-home.

Running behind? Cut in this order

  1. Step 4 (join): one sentence, keep it as a stretch
  2. Step 3: run only “what can't this data answer?”
  3. Dashboard: open the backup instead of generating
  4. Never cut Step 2's “what did it miss?” or the H6 summary

Answer keys

Computed in code from the files and re-checked by cleaning the raw files a second, independent way; both agree to the cent. Cleaning rules: standardize names; move misfiled items; remove the exact duplicate; blank discounts = 0; exclude dates outside Jan–Jun 2026; fix the price typo; recalculate Net_Sales = Price_USD × Quantity × (1 − Discount_Pct/100). Average discount = simple average per sale. Gross margin = Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales.
